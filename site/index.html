Methods and limitations

Flagging rule
A patient is flagged when their risk score is at or above the threshold (0.147, set on training patients to flag about 10%).
Confidence intervals
95% stratified bootstrap (1000 resamples, seed 0): patients are resampled within each group and outcome.
Reference group
The largest group in each column; gaps are group minus reference.
Small groups
Flagged, not dropped, when they have fewer than 30 readmitted or fewer than 30 not-readmitted patients.
Metrics
AUC: how well scores rank readmitted above not-readmitted patients (0.5 = chance). Brier: average squared error of the risk (lower is better). O/E: observed ÷ expected readmissions (1 = calibrated; above 1 = risk underestimated). FPR: flagged but not readmitted. FNR: readmitted but not flagged. PPV: flagged patients who were readmitted.
What this cannot tell you
Why a gap exists, whether the labels themselves are biased (who gets readmitted depends on access to care), or how the model performs on other hospitals' patients. Race, sex and age are audit variables, not model inputs.
